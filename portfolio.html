§ 01

Abstract — how a nose changed my life

In high school, I co-authored peer-reviewed research with IIT Roorkee: a machine learning model that tracks anatomical landmarks in rhinoplasty images. It hit 92% accuracy, picked up 5+ citations on Google Scholar, and permanently set my direction — build AI that actually helps in medicine.

Now I'm at Waterloo (Faculty of Math) in the CS co-op program. My current co-op is at a healthcare AI company, working on an AI assistant that untangles drug formularies and prior authorization — one of healthcare's most frustrating workflows, which is exactly why it's worth fixing.

Outside lectures: daily LeetCode in C, sorted by pattern, pushed to GitHub. Pointers don't scare me anymore. Mostly.

Plate II · The origin story

AI-Powered Rhinoplasty Assessment System

An end-to-end pipeline that detects 9 nose-specific anatomical landmarks across 250 medical images at 92% accuracy, cutting clinician assessment time. Facial landmarks (dlib) become 36 perceptual distance features, feeding a 100-tree random forest. Co-authored with IIT Roorkee, peer-reviewed, published, and cited 5+ times on Google Scholar — all before my first university lecture.

Plate III · From the lab notebook

AI Music Genre Classifier

A 4-layer CNN (~150K parameters) that classifies Rock, Classical, and Jazz from mel-spectrograms — it never hears the music, it just reads it like an image. Batch norm + dropout, trains in ~2 minutes on CPU. Currently trained on generated audio; real-dataset training (GTZAN) is next on the roadmap.

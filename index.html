§ 01

Abstract: how a nose changed my life

In high school, I co-authored peer-reviewed research with IIT Roorkee and AIIMS Rishikesh, published in the Journal of Plastic, Reconstructive & Aesthetic Surgery: a mathematical method that uses AI facial-landmark detection to put an objective number on nasal deformity from a single photo. Tested on 250 patient images, it picked up 5+ citations on Google Scholar and permanently set my direction: build AI that actually helps in medicine.

Now I'm at Waterloo (Faculty of Math) in the CS co-op program. My current co-op is at a healthcare AI company, working on an AI assistant that untangles drug formularies and prior authorization (one of healthcare's most frustrating workflows), which is exactly why it's worth fixing.

Outside lectures: daily LeetCode in C, sorted by pattern, pushed to GitHub. Pointers don't scare me anymore. Mostly.

Plate II · The origin story

AI-Powered Rhinoplasty Assessment System

A computer-vision method that quantifies nasal deformity from a single 2D photo. A landmark model pre-trained on iBUG-300W locates the 9 nose points; the 36 distances between them are min-max normalised and scored as z-scores against ~250 post-rhinoplasty images (male patients, 18 to 24) to give a defect score Q from 0 to 100%. It averaged 76.2% across the dataset, giving surgeons an objective measure for preoperative planning. Co-authored with researchers at IIT Roorkee and AIIMS Rishikesh, published in JPRAS (Journal of Plastic, Reconstructive & Aesthetic Surgery, 2024), and cited 5+ times on Google Scholar, all before my first university lecture.

1Nine points, 36 distances

31 distances used 5 excluded as low-confidence (points 32–36 along the base)

2Normalise, then standardise
  1. nij = minmax(dij)Min-max scale every distance so photo size, lighting and lens don’t matter.
  2. kij = (nij − μij) / σijHow many standard deviations each distance sits from the ~250 post-surgery images.
  3. z+, z− = table(mean k+, mean k−)Average the positive and the negative shifts, then look each up in a z-table.
  4. Q = max(z+, z−) × 1000 to 100%. Higher means a larger defect.

Plate III · Summer 2026

ESCI Semantic Product Search

A product-search system on Amazon’s ESCI shopping-queries benchmark, built as a measurement project first. BM25, dense retrieval (bge-small + FAISS), rank fusion and a cross-encoder reranker are each scored on the full 8,956-query test split, so every quality gain is priced in latency. From there: an HNSW index swap, a FastAPI service with memory and load testing, LLM query rewriting (better quality, but not serving-viable as built), and a hard-negative fine-tune of the embedding model that beat the old best pipeline at 2.6× the speed.

Plate IV · From the lab notebook

AI Music Genre Classifier

A small CNN (three conv blocks, 101,571 parameters) that classifies Rock, Classical, and Jazz from spectrograms: it never hears the music, it just reads it like an image. Batch norm + dropout, and the whole run, data generation included, takes about 40 seconds on a laptop CPU. It’s trained on synthetic tones, so 100% on its 18 held-out samples proves the pipeline, not the music; real-dataset training (GTZAN) is next on the roadmap.

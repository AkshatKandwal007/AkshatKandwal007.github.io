Abstract: how a nose changed my life

In high school, I co-authored peer-reviewed research with researchers at IIT Roorkee and AIIMS Rishikesh: a statistical method that quantifies nasal deformity from facial-landmark distances in rhinoplasty patients. It was published in JPRAS, picked up 5+ citations on Google Scholar, and permanently set my direction: build AI that actually helps in medicine.

Now I'm at Waterloo (Faculty of Math) in the CS co-op program. My current co-op is at a healthcare AI company, working on an AI assistant that untangles drug formularies and prior authorization (one of healthcare's most frustrating workflows), which is exactly why it's worth fixing.

Outside lectures: daily LeetCode in C, sorted by pattern, pushed to GitHub. Pointers don't scare me anymore. Mostly.

Plate II · The origin story

Quantifying Nasal Deformity in Rhinoplasty

A statistical method for quantifying nasal deformity before rhinoplasty: a pre-trained facial-landmark model (iBUG-300W) locates 9 nose-specific points across 250 pre- and post-surgery patient photos (RealSelf, ages 18–24), producing 36 pairwise distances per face. Min–max normalising each distance against the full dataset and scoring each patient's deviation from the population mean/median gives a single defect-quantification score — 76.2% on average across the cohort. Co-authored with researchers at IIT Roorkee and AIIMS Rishikesh, peer-reviewed, published in JPRAS, and cited 5+ times on Google Scholar, all before my first university lecture.
